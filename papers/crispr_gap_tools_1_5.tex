\begin{abstract}
Public CRISPR tooling concentrates on a narrow slice of the editing problem.
We attack five evidence-backed gaps with working tools benchmarked on real
public data: (1) cross-dataset collapse of guide efficacy models, which we
quantify on Doench-2016 FC+RES, Doench V1 and the DeepHF U2OS screen;
(2) absence of calibrated probabilities from off-target scorers, addressed
with isotonic/Platt calibration on 25,632 crisprSQL measurements;
(3) pegRNA data scarcity, attacked with transfer learning from Cas9 efficacy
data to the 22,956-pegRNA PRIDICT2 dataset; (4) PAM-variant-blind off-target
scoring; (5) chromatin-context-blind off-target scoring. All claims are
bounded by held-out, group-aware evaluation; negative results are reported.
Beyond the five gap tools, the analysis surfaces a \emph{discovery}: a
target-inherent transfer residual -- the per-guide error of a
source-trained model replicates at $\rho=0.86$--$0.90$ across three
independent nuclease screens ($n=53{,}937$ matched guides), more stably
than the biological signal itself ($\rho\approx0.73$). The residual is
predictable from sequence and chromatin features and yields \textbf{PORTA},
a portability correction that rescues cross-dataset transfer from
$\rho=0.015$ to $0.522$ (SpCas9-HF1) and $0.025$ to $0.560$ (eSpCas9)
with zero destination labels, and exposes a per-guide portability score
no current tool reports. On calibrated off-target scoring, five fully-seeded
guide-grouped splits of crisprSQL yield a meta-finding: every model's
AUROC swings by ${\sim}0.2$ across splits and inits (GNN $0.45$--$0.69$),
dwarfing model gaps -- single-split published comparisons on
negative-scarce corpora are irreproducible by construction. The strongest model is a
position-specific logistic regression ($0.692 \pm 0.154$), beating the
GNN ($0.609 \pm 0.084$) on 4/5 identical splits; no deep model
separates from the classical baselines (CFD $0.552$, MIT $0.516$). A natural per-study routing hypothesis (GNN for
low-prevalence GUIDE-seq-style studies, MIT otherwise) is \emph{falsified}
under leave-one-out evaluation: the apparent per-study GNN wins rest on as
few as two positives, and the robust policy on this corpus is the MIT
baseline everywhere (Section~6). All artifacts -- code, 67-test offline suite, data pipelines,
figures, and this paper's numbers -- regenerate from committed scripts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Computational CRISPR tooling has converged on a narrow problem shape:
SpCas9 on-target efficacy in a handful of human cell lines, and off-target
\emph{ranking} scores for NGG-PAM targets. Auditing the public tool and
dataset landscape surfaces five gaps, each backed by dataset-level evidence:
(1)~efficacy models are rarely evaluated across independent assays, and
reported performance collapses when they are; (2)~the widely used MIT and
CFD off-target scores are uncalibrated ranking heuristics -- no published
tool returns a probability of cleavage with an error bound; (3)~prime-editing
pegRNA design models are data-scarce relative to Cas9 nuclease models by two
orders of magnitude (22,956 measured pegRNAs vs.\ millions of Cas9 guide
measurements), yet transfer from Cas9 data is essentially untried in public
tools; (4)~off-target scorers are trained and validated almost exclusively on
NGG-PAM off-targets even though 47\% of measured off-targets in crisprSQL
carry non-NGG PAMs; (5)~chromatin context, known to modulate cleavage
genome-wide, is absent from the standard sequence-only scoring stack despite
crisprSQL shipping CTCF, DNase, RRBS, H3K4me3 and DRIP tracks for every one
of its 25,632 measured pairs. We build one tool per gap and report honest,
group-aware held-out numbers, including where the gap resisted us.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i)~A measurement harness for cross-dataset generalization of efficacy
models, with the first cross-family transfer matrix on Doench/DeepHF
(Section~5). (ii)~A calibrated multi-study off-target benchmark with full
split/init determinism, establishing that negative-scarce corpora cannot
adjudicate between scorers on single splits -- and that per-study
apparent wins, including our own, are positive-count artifacts (a
study-routing hypothesis is stated and falsified, Section~6). (iii)~A controlled study of
Cas9$\to$pegRNA transfer, establishing an honest negative at three
scales in two cell lines (Section~7). (iv)~PAM- and chromatin-aware
off-target channels; five-seed replication reverses our own single-split
finding -- no channel's delta exceeds split noise, and PAM alone
\emph{hurts} on average (Section~8). (v)~A named, quantified, falsifiable discovery --
the target-inherent transfer residual -- and PORTA, the correction it
enables (Section~12). (vi)~A reproducible codebase: 40 hermetic tests,
public data only, every figure regenerated from result files (Appendix~A).

\section{Gap 1: Cross-dataset generalization of efficacy models}
\begin{definition}[Cross-dataset generalization gap]
Let $\mathcal{D}_A, \mathcal{D}_B$ be guide-efficacy datasets with score
distributions $P_A, P_B$, and $f_A$ a model trained on $\mathcal{D}_A$ with
held-out Spearman $\rho_{AA}$ and transfer Spearman $\rho_{AB}$. The
generalization gap is $g(A\!\to\!B) = \rho_{AA} - \rho_{AB}$.
\end{definition}
\begin{proposition}[Identity of indiscernibles for rank transfer]\label{prop:indisc}
If $P_B$ is an affine rescaling of the conditional score law of $P_A$, i.e.\
$Y_B = aY_A + b$ a.s.\ with $a>0$, then $g(A\!\to\!B) = 0$ for any model
$f_A$ that is optimal under squared loss on $\mathcal{D}_A$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Spearman correlation is invariant under strictly increasing transformations
of either argument. If $Y_B = aY_A+b$ with $a>0$ then
$\rho(f_A(X), Y_B) = \rho(f_A(X), aY_A+b) = \rho(f_A(X), Y_A)$, and an
$P_A$-optimal predictor attains the same rank correlation on $B$ as on $A$,
so $\rho_{AB}=\rho_{AA}$ and $g=0$.
\end{proof}
The contrapositive is the empirical content of the gap: observed
$g \gg 0$ certifies that the conditional score laws differ beyond affine
rescaling (different assay, cell type, or delivery), which is what our
harness measures (Section~4.1).

\section{Gap 2: Calibrated cleavage probabilities}
Published scores (MIT/Hsu-2013, CFD/Doench-2016) are ranking scores, not
probabilities. We fit monotone calibrators $c: \mathbb{R}\to[0,1]$ on a
held-out validation slice and evaluate with expected calibration error
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{ECE} = \sum_{m=1}^{M} \frac{|B_m|}{n}\, \big|\,\mathrm{acc}(B_m) - \mathrm{conf}(B_m)\,\big|,
\end{equation}
over equal-width bins $B_m$, and the Brier score
$\mathrm{BS} = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i (p_i - y_i)^2$.
\begin{proposition}[Brier decomposition implies ECE lower bound]
For any binned predictor with per-bin confidence $\bar p_m$ and accuracy
$\bar y_m$, $\mathrm{BS} \ge \sum_m \frac{|B_m|}{n}(\bar y_m - \bar p_m)^2$,
hence calibration error contributes quadratically to Brier while ECE
measures it in absolute terms.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The Murphy decomposition gives
$\mathrm{BS} = \sum_m \frac{|B_m|}{n}(\bar y_m - \bar p_m)^2
+ \sum_m \frac{|B_m|}{n}\,\mathrm{Var}(p \mid B_m)
- \mathrm{Var}(Y)$ up to the standard reliability--resolution--uncertainty
terms; the reliability term is exactly the sum of squared per-bin
calibration gaps, and variance terms are nonnegative.
\end{proof}

\section{Gap 3: Transfer learning for pegRNA efficiency}
% fully-convolutional trunk; why length-agnostic weight transfer is valid
The conv trunk applies the same filters $\{W_k\}$ to any input length $L$:
$h^{(L)} = \phi(W * x^{(L)})$, and global average pooling
$\bar h = \frac{1}{L}\sum_t h_t$ makes the head length-agnostic. Pretraining
on 30-mer Cas9 contexts and fine-tuning on 99-mer pegRNA contexts therefore
transfers motif detectors without architectural surgery.

\section{Gaps 4 and 5: PAM-variant and chromatin-aware off-target scoring}
We extend a message-passing duplex GNN with a global channel
$g_i = [\,\mathrm{onehot}(\mathrm{PAM}_i);\ \mathbf{e}_i\,]$ concatenated
after pooling, where $\mathbf{e}_i\in\mathbb{R}^5$ is the crisprSQL
epigenetic track vector. The ablation
$\{\varnothing, \mathrm{PAM}, \mathbf{e}, \mathrm{PAM}{+}\mathbf{e}\}$
isolates each channel's contribution on an identical guide-grouped split.
For gap 4 the decisive slice is the non-NGG held-out subset: if PAM
awareness only memorized majority-PAM statistics, non-NGG AUROC would be
unchanged; an increase localizes the feature's value to PAM-variant
contexts, where SpG/SpRY-class editors operate.
\begin{proposition}[Pooling preserves mismatch interaction signals]
Let $G$ be the duplex graph with backbone edges and all-pairs mismatch
edges, and $h_v^{(T)}$ the node states after $T$ message-passing steps with
a GRU update. Then for any two positions $u,v$ carrying mismatches, $h_u$
and $h_v$ are functions of each other's input features for $T\ge 1$, so
mean/max-pooled readout retains mismatch--mismatch interaction information
that position-independent scoring rules (MIT, CFD) discard by construction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Mismatch nodes are mutually adjacent by construction. One message-passing
step aggregates neighbor messages at every node, so $h_v^{(1)}$ depends on
$x_u$ for every mismatch neighbor $u$; induction extends this to $T>1$.
Mean and max pooling are permutation-invariant functions of
$\{h_v^{(T)}\}$, hence retain these joint features. MIT scores factor as
$\prod_i (1-w_i)$ over mismatch positions and CFD as a product of
per-position terms, so neither can represent any interaction term.
\end{proof}

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Datasets}
All datasets are public and pulled by \texttt{scripts/fetch\_data.sh} or
vendored mirrors; no live calls occur inside the test suite.
\textbf{Doench FC+RES} (5,310 30-mer guide contexts; Azimuth mirror of
Doench et al.\ 2016) with \texttt{score\_drug\_gene\_rank} efficacy in
$[0,1]$. \textbf{Doench V1} (2,144 mouse guides; 34-mer extended contexts
trimmed to the Azimuth 30-mer convention) with Percent Rank efficacy.
\textbf{DeepHF} (55,604 guides with indel frequencies for WT-SpCas9 in
U2OS; Wang et al.\ 2019) as the divergent assay family. \textbf{crisprSQL}
100720 release (25,632 experimentally measured guide/off-target pairs from
17 studies, with cleavage frequencies, five epigenetic tracks,
thermodynamic energies, cell line and study provenance; St\"ortz \&
Minary 2021). \textbf{PRIDICT2 23k} (22,956 pegRNAs with 99-mer wide
target contexts, engineered PBS/RT features and measured editing
efficiencies in HEK293T and K562; Koeppel et al.\ 2023, underlying screens
from Mathis et al.\ 2023).
\subsection{Models and protocols}
Efficacy models: ridge on one-hot+dinucleotide+GC features, and a
fully-convolutional CNN (two Conv1d layers, global mean+max pooling).
Off-target models: a duplex message-passing GNN (base-pair identity,
mismatch flag and position node features; backbone plus all-pairs mismatch
edges; GRU updates) with optional global PAM/epigenetic channels.
Baselines: MIT/Hsu-2013 and CFD/Doench-2016 reimplemented from the primary
sources (CFD via the published CRISPOR reference matrices). Every split is
group-aware (by guide for off-target, by pegRNA group for gap 3) so no
near-duplicate leaks between train and test; calibration is fit only on a
validation slice; test sets keep natural class prevalence so ECE is
meaningful. Hyperparameters are fixed before evaluation; seeds are
reported with every number.
\subsection{Reproduction}
\texttt{python -m crisprgap.train\_efficacy},
\texttt{python -m crisprgap.train\_offtarget},
\texttt{python -m crisprgap.train\_offtarget\_ablation},
\texttt{python -m crisprgap.train\_pegrna}; harnesses in
\texttt{crisprgap/crossdata.py} and \texttt{scripts/run\_crossdata\_cnn.py}.
The hermetic suite (\texttt{pytest}) runs 40 tests offline.

\subsection{Gap 1: collapse across divergent assays, transfer within families}

\label{fig:matrix}

Ridge models trained on Doench FC+RES transfer to Doench V1 at
$\rho=0.501$ (within: $0.455$) and V1$\to$FC+RES at $0.434$ (within:
$0.458$): inside the Doench assay family the score laws are near

bottleneck is therefore not model capacity or feature quality but assay
distribution shift -- the quantified form of the gap (Figure~\ref{fig:crossdata},
Table~\ref{tab:crossdata}). A CNN under a tight compute budget shows the
same structure, and something stronger: transfer to DeepHF goes
\emph{negative} ($\rho=-0.25$ from both Doench datasets,
Table~\ref{tab:crosscnn}), i.e.\ a model confident on Doench is
anti-predictive on the divergent assay rather than merely uninformative.
Within-dataset CNN numbers are undertrained at this budget and are reported
as such.

\caption{Within-dataset vs cross-dataset Spearman (ridge, 3 seeds).}

\subsection{Gap 1 (cont.): pooling does not fix the boundary}
Naive multi-dataset pooling fails both ways: Doench-pooled training still
transfers to DeepHF at only $\rho=0.014$, and adding Doench data to DeepHF
training slightly \emph{hurts} held-out DeepHF ($0.68$--$0.70$ pooled vs.\
$0.72$--$0.73$ pure, 3 seeds). The assay-family boundary, not dataset size,
is the binding constraint (Table~\ref{tab:pooled}).

\subsection{Gap 2: determinism audit and the split-variance meta-finding}

\begin{figure}[h]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{figs/fig_offtarget_roc_cal.png}
\caption{Gap 2, canonical fully-seeded split of crisprSQL. Left: ROC for
the GNN and the MIT score. Right: calibration before/after isotonic
regression on the small validation slice.}
\label{fig:roc_cal}
\end{figure}
\label{sec:results-offtarget}
\begin{figure}[h]\centering\includegraphics[width=0.5\textwidth]{fig_calibration.pdf}
\caption{Reliability curves on held-out crisprSQL pairs, before and after
Platt calibration on the validation slice.}
\label{fig:calibration}\end{figure}
We rebuilt this benchmark for full determinism (seeded torch init after
catching unseeded-run variance) and ran five fully-seeded guide-grouped
splits (held-out pair counts 487--1,079, natural prevalence).
Table~\ref{tab:offtarget} gives the matrix: the GNN averages
$0.609 \pm 0.084$ against CFD $0.552 \pm 0.094$ and MIT
$0.516 \pm 0.119$, leading on mean but winning only 3/5 and 2/5 of
splits respectively, with every model's AUROC ranging ${\sim}0.2$ across
splits and inits (GNN $0.454$--$0.685$). On the canonical seed-0 split,
the paired bootstrap interval for the GNN-minus-MIT difference includes
zero ($-0.043$ [$-0.115$, $+0.025$]). \textbf{Meta-finding: on
negative-scarce multi-study corpora, split and init variance dwarfs
model gaps -- single-split published comparisons on such corpora are
irreproducible by construction.} Calibration is equally unstable:
isotonic recalibration on each split's small validation slice raises mean
ECE ($0.326 \to 0.389$, helping only 1 of 5 splits), reversing the
textbook expectation at this sample size (Table~\ref{tab:calib}).
\paragraph{A one-hot logistic beats the GNN.}
The canonical strong-but-simple baseline for this task -- logistic
regression on one-hot guide$\times$off-target base pairings at each
position -- reaches $0.692 \pm 0.154$ on the identical five splits,
beating the GNN on 4/5 (paired mean $+0.083$), CFD on 4/5 and MIT on
4/5, with per-seed AUROC spanning $0.446$--$0.930$. The strongest model
on crisprSQL is therefore not the GNN but a linear model over positional
mismatch indicators, and the entire model ordering is small beside the
split-to-split swing ($0.484$ within the logistic model alone). This
strengthens the meta-finding: on negative-scarce corpora, model choice is
second-order to evaluation design.
\paragraph{Init-only versus split variance.}
Retraining the GNN on the \emph{identical} seed-0 split with five torch
init draws (data order fixed) gives AUROC $0.530$--$0.606$ (sd $0.032$)
and uncalibrated ECE $0.209$--$0.389$: initialization alone contributes
over a third of the total split-plus-init spread (sd $0.032$ of $0.084$)
and swings calibration error by $0.18$ on byte-identical data. ECE
comparisons between calibration methods on single inits are therefore
uninterpretable at this scale.

Per-study analysis on the seed-0 split (Table~\ref{tab:perstudy})
initially suggested structure -- a GNN win on Anderson-era GUIDE-seq data
($0.764$ vs MIT $0.550$) and a loss on Kim-style targeted libraries
($0.487$ vs $0.692$). \textbf{This structure does not survive audit.}
The Anderson row rests on \emph{two} held-out positives. Across the five
seeded splits, only 13 (study, split) pairs carry at least 30 test pairs
with $\ge$5 positives and $\ge$5 negatives; the GNN wins exactly one of
them. A leave-one-out logistic router on study features (size, prevalence,
mismatch load, PAM mix, GC) collapses to always-MIT (routed AUROC
$0.6283$ $=$ always-MIT $0.6283$; bootstrap 95\% CI for the difference
$[0,0]$), an explicit ``GNN when prevalence $<0.10$'' rule does
\emph{worse} than always-MIT ($0.609$), and even an oracle router gains
only $0.001$ -- there is no routing headroom on this corpus. We therefore
report the study-routing direction as falsified: per-study model rankings
on crisprSQL are not estimable at current positive counts (1,494 validated
negatives across 17 studies), which is itself the corpus's core
limitation. Table~\ref{tab:routing} lists every audited row.

\subsection{Gap 3: Cas9-to-pegRNA transfer}
\begin{figure}[h]\centering\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{fig_pegrna_scaling.pdf}
\caption{Scarce-regime scaling: Cas9-motif transfer never beats scratch.}
\label{fig:scaling}\end{figure}
On the PRIDICT2 23k library (grouped held-out pegRNAs, HEK293T), a CNN over
the 99-mer target context beats the engineered-feature ridge baseline
($\rho=0.794$ vs.\ $0.741$). Transferring the Doench-pretrained conv trunk
does \emph{not} beat training from scratch at this scale ($0.789$ vs.\
$0.794$).
\subsubsection{Scarce-regime scaling: a clean negative}
Subsampling pegRNA training data to the truly scarce regime does not rescue
the transfer hypothesis: at $n=250$ scratch reaches $\rho=0.725$ vs.\
transfer $0.724$; at $n=1000$, $0.762$ vs.\ $0.758$; at $n=4000$, $0.771$
vs.\ $0.767$. Cas9 guide-efficacy motifs do not transfer to pegRNA
efficiency at any measured scale. The interpretation is mechanistic: pegRNA
efficiency is dominated by PBS/RT-template geometry and edit context (the
numeric channel alone already gives $0.741$), not by the protospacer motifs
that Cas9-efficacy pretraining encodes. Two findings survive the negative:
(i)~sequence-context CNNs are strong pegRNA predictors even with 250
training examples ($\rho=0.72$), and (ii)~the field's assumption that
prime-editing models should borrow from nuclease models needs evidence, not
analogy. The K562 track replicates the pattern (ridge $0.554$, scratch
$0.610$, transfer $0.588$). A second negative: folding all 22{,}956
mutated target contexts with ViennaRNA RNAfold (40-nt edit-centered
window) and adding the MFE feature to the numeric ridge changes grouped
held-out Spearman from $0.7329$ to $0.7331$. Two further thermodynamic
channels confirm the null: seqfold minimum-ensemble $\Delta G$ on the same
40-nt edit-centered window and primer3 nearest-neighbor protospacer $T_m$
move grouped held-out Spearman by $-6\times10^{-5}$ (HEK) and
$-1\times10^{-4}$ (K562) across three grouped splits
($n=22{,}956$). Three independent thermodynamic/structural tools, zero
signal -- local target secondary structure, at least at this resolution,
is not the missing signal. A final control reframes the gap-3 story: a
LightGBM gradient-boosted model on the \emph{19 engineered numeric
features alone} matches the sequence CNN (HEK $0.795$ vs $0.794$; K562
$0.610$ vs $0.610$, three grouped splits each). The wide-target sequence
channel therefore adds nothing beyond what the engineered edit-geometry
features already encode; SHAP attribution ranks Correction\_Replacement,
Correction\_Length and the RT/PBS melting-temperature features highest,
consistent with the mechanistic reading that edit geometry, not
protospacer motif, drives pegRNA efficiency. Tuning the GBM (30 Optuna
TPE trials on the seed-0 train/val split only) pushes it \emph{past} the
CNN: HEK $0.798$ vs $0.794$, K562 $0.616$ vs $0.610$ (three grouped
splits, frozen config). The strongest model we benchmark for pegRNA
efficacy is therefore a tuned tabular model over engineered edit-geometry
features -- the sequence CNN's advantage is an artifact of comparing
against an untuned baseline. The conclusion is library-consistent: XGBoost
in the same tuned hyperparameter region reproduces it (HEK $0.799$, K562
$0.606$), while \emph{default} XGBoost collapses (HEK $0.742$, K562
$0.476$) -- a 0.06--0.13 Spearman swing from tuning alone, larger than any
architecture difference we measured on this task. We preserve all negatives as
the gap-3 result.

\section{Discovery: target-inherent transfer residual}
\label{sec:discovery}
Training on Doench FC+RES and scoring the DeepHF guides yields a per-guide
transfer residual (predicted rank minus measured rank). Across the three
independent nuclease screens of the same ${\sim}54$k guides, the residuals
replicate at $\rho=0.86$ (WT vs eSpCas9), $0.90$ (eSpCas9 vs SpCas9-HF1)
and $0.86$ (WT vs HF1), while the measured efficacies themselves correlate
at only ${\sim}0.73$: \textbf{the model's transfer error is more stable
across nucleases than the biological signal.} The residual is therefore a
target-inherent property (sequence and/or locus context), not a nuclease or
model artifact. A feature decomposition finds no single dominant driver
(max $|\rho|=0.28$ over the 11 DeepHF biofeatures; GC $0.25$), i.e.\ a
distributed sequence-composition signature. Folding all guides with
ViennaRNA adds a twelfth candidate driver and it too fails:
guide+PAM minimum free energy correlates with the residual at only
$\rho=-0.085$, and appending it to the portability model moves
cross-screen prediction by $+0.0002$. Falsification was built in: the
third screen (HF1) was held out of the initial analysis and confirmed the
concordance. Two consequences: (i)~cross-assay portability is a measurable,
predictable property of a guide and can be reported as a design score --
no current tool exposes it; (ii)~transfer-failure analyses that blame
``model overfitting'' are incomplete: a large, stable, target-inherent
component sits in the assay shift itself. A companion attempt to turn
crisprSQL's non-NGG pairs into an empirical PAM-activity atlas that beats
CFD failed its out-of-sample test (mean guide-held-out $\rho$: atlas
$0.464$ vs CFD $0.469$) and is preserved as a negative
(\texttt{results/pam\_atlas\_falsification.json}).

\subsection{Portability correction: rescuing cross-dataset transfer}
\label{sec:porta}
If the residual is target-inherent and predictable, it should be
\emph{correctable}. We test this directly. A ridge \emph{portability model}
is fit on the WT-SpCas9 screen only: inputs are the guide's sequence
features and DeepHF biofeatures, and the target is the per-guide transfer
residual (rank of the Doench-FC+RES prediction minus rank of the measured
WT activity). The model never sees any label from the destination screen.
Applied zero-shot to SpCas9-HF1, it predicts the per-guide residual at
$\rho=0.781$ on all 56{,}888 guides and $\rho=0.700$ on the 2{,}609 guides
whose sequences never occur in the WT training screen. Subtracting the
predicted residual from the raw Doench transfer ranking lifts transfer
performance from $\rho=0.015$ (effectively zero) to $\rho=0.522$; on
strictly unseen guides it goes from $-0.019$ (anti-correlated) to $0.359$.
On eSpCas9 the same WT-trained model lifts transfer from $0.025$ to
$0.560$ (n$=$58{,}617). Corrected performance approaches the within-screen
CNN baseline ($0.55$--$0.61$), i.e.\ the correction recovers most of the
signal that domain shift destroys. This is a new capability: Rule Set 3,
CRISPRon, and DeepHF-class models are all single-domain; none reports or
exploits a per-guide portability score. We expose it as the
\texttt{crisprgap} portability tool: given a guide set and one calibration
screen, it outputs a per-guide portability score and a corrected ranking.

\section{Limitations}
crisprSQL aggregates 17 studies with different assays and sensitivities;
its 94\% positive prevalence and 1,494 validated negatives cap
discrimination and make cross-study confounds unavoidable (we split by
guide\,$\times$\,study to bound leakage). Our off-target AUROCs
($0.51$--$0.58$) are honest for this split but below what single-study
evaluations report; we claim superiority only over MIT/CFD measured on the
identical split. The pegRNA transfer negative is established for
sequence-motif transfer from SpCas9 efficacy; parameter transfer from
larger prime-editing-specific pretraining remains open. Epigenetic tracks
in crisprSQL are study-confounded. Models are intentionally small
(sandbox-scale); scaling laws are out of scope.

\section{Tool documentation}
Each gap ships as a callable tool: \texttt{crisprgap.crossdata} (gap 1
harness), \texttt{crisprgap.calibration} + \texttt{crisprgap.cfd} (gap 2
calibrated scorer with MIT/CFD baselines), \texttt{crisprgap.train\_pegrna}
+ \texttt{crisprgap.models.transfer\_cnn} (gap 3 predictor),
\texttt{crisprgap.models.offtarget\_v2} (gaps 4--5 PAM/chromatin-aware
scorer). All tools are covered by the hermetic pytest suite.

\subsection{PORTA: portability scoring and corrected transfer}
\label{sec:porta-tool}
PORTA packages the discovery as a design tool. Inputs: a source-trained
efficacy model $f$, one \emph{calibration screen} measuring a guide library
(any public screen whose guides overlap the target library), and a set of
query guides with sequences and optional biofeatures. Outputs per guide:
(i)~the \emph{portability score} $\hat r(g) = m(\phi(g), b(g))$ -- the
predicted normalized-rank residual of $f$ on the calibration screen --
and (ii)~the corrected ranking $\tilde p(g) = \mathrm{nrank}(f(g)) -
\hat r(g)$. Interpretation: guides in the top PORTA tail are ones the
source model systematically over-ranks \emph{for assay reasons}, so their
low transfer rank should not disqualify them; the bottom tail flags
guides likely to disappoint on a new assay despite high source scores.
Reference usage:
\begin{verbatim}
from crisprgap.porta import fit_portability, correct
m = fit_portability(model, calib_screen)      # ridge on rank residuals
score = m.score(seqs, biofeatures)            # per-guide portability
ranking = correct(model, seqs, biofeatures)   # corrected ranks
\end{verbatim}
Validation for this exact configuration (Doench source, DeepHF WT
calibration, HF1/eSpCas9 destination) is Table~9; the correction is
exact when residual replication and portability prediction are perfect
(Proposition~\ref{prop:porta}) and degrades gracefully with their
product.
